Each point is a price bucket. The x-axis shows the probability the market assigns; the y-axis shows how often markets in that bucket actually resolved Yes. The dashed line marks perfect calibration. Points that sit far above or below it mark ranges the market priced wrong. Filled dot = statistically significant (n≥30, 95% CI). Open ring = still collecting data.

ARB rows show cases where buying every mutually exclusive outcome at the current best ask, including each market's real taker fee where one applies, still costs less than $1.00 total. That's mathematically riskless, but still not a guaranteed profit (slippage and thin liquidity can still eat into it, since prices can move before an order fills). CAL rows work differently: they flag price ranges where past resolved markets show a statistically significant gap between price and outcome, with each market's real taker fee folded into the cost side of that edge, same as ARB. No single bet is guaranteed; the edge shows up over many repeated positions. MIS rows list markets with at least $5,000 in 24-hour volume where the current price differs from that bucket's historical resolution rate by 15 fee-adjusted points or more, with no statistical-significance requirement. None of this is investment advice.
